# Show both rulers before the sentence ends

The hook should open on the two AI rulers already doing their thing, with the voice labelling what the picture proves, and it should not open on a presenter explaining the premise. Frame one is a finished poster: an orator mid-gesture, a dancer mid-move, two countries, readable on mute. The voice still starts in the first second, because that is the only opening feature with field data behind it: speech in the first 3 seconds is tied to **+11.7% retention at 3 s and +24.7% at 10 s** over music-only openings ([Emplifi](https://emplifi.io/resources/facebook-reels-data/)). What changes is the picture under the voice. The leaked MrBeast production memo states the rule directly, "Stop telling people what they will be watching and start showing them", and its documented openings pair a first shot that matches the thumbnail with a first sentence that matches the title ([memo mirror](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)). The premium feel then comes from a small set of motion-based devices that need no brightness change at all: a camera that is already moving on frame one, a cut hidden in a whip, a 2-3 frame hold on contact, a small decaying shake, world-built dust, and a sound hit on the contact frame. Two limits apply to everything below. Almost no source gives numbers for camera moves, so most parameters are working starting values and are marked as such. And no shot-by-shot breakdown of any "AI runs a country" or civilization-experiment opening was found, so the second-by-second builds are designs derived from the sourced rules, not copies of proven videos.

## The premise has to be visible in frame one, with the voice as its label

### What the sourced rules say

The best-documented opening construction is a two-step: the first shot matches the packaging and the first sentence matches the title. A third-party breakdown of MrBeast's videos describes "Ages 1 Through 100" opening on the thumbnail image, stating the age range in the first line, and starting the challenge at about 12 seconds; the Antarctica intro runs **8 shots in 20 seconds** ([SozAI transcript](https://sozai.app/transcript/mrbeast-solved-youtube/)). That is long-form YouTube and a secondhand source, so the timings do not transfer. The structure does: one shot per element of the premise.

Game-trailer editor Derek Lieu supplies the test for whether an opening works. A cold open is a self-contained 5-10 seconds that must raise a question or start mid-idea, and slow shots of environment art leave "no story hook holding the audience in suspense" ([Derek Lieu, 2019](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2019/10/2/editing-an-engaging-trailer-opening)). His six cold-open types (bumper, scene lift, flashback, rug pull, name reveal, enigma) are the most concrete written taxonomy found. For his 2026 Mandrake trailer he opened on the strangest image the game owned while the same shot kept the genre readable ([Derek Lieu, 2026](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2026/4/3/mandrake-announce-trailer)). For this reel the Roblox look does the genre job with no words, and the dancing head of state is the strange image.

Shorts creator Jenny Hoyos adds the mute rule: the hook must be understandable without sound, and the opening frame should work as a title plus thumbnail. Her figures come from interview digests and conflict on timing, so treat them as direction ([Briefread summary](https://briefread.pages.dev/en/summary/meet-the-youtuber-who-solved-shorts)). The earlier retention research in this project puts the muted share in context: **over 75% of Reels views are sound-on** ([Meta for Developers](https://developers.facebook.com/blog/post/2024/11/07/unlock-the-power-of-reel-ads/)). The mute test is therefore a clarity test for the picture more than a rescue for silent viewers.

### Show the state, withhold the outcome

Two pieces of advice appear to clash. The memo says to front-load the interesting material and, for multi-day videos, not to start on day one ([memo mirror](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)). Spotter's creator team says to tease without revealing, because showing the climax removes the reason to stay ([Spotter](https://spottercommunity.beehiiv.com/p/creative-effective-video-intros-hook-viewers)). Every concrete example resolves the same way: show the extreme state, hold back who wins. The fireworks video shows every firework and not the biggest explosion. Lab work on curiosity agrees, since curiosity peaks when the viewer knows enough to see exactly what is missing ([Journal of Cognition](https://journalofcognition.org/articles/10.5334/joc.501)). For this reel the state is "an AI orator and an AI dancer each run a country", and the withheld outcome is which country does better.

### Where the presenter and the text go

The earlier research ranked "open on the presenter large, close and facing camera" as a weak-evidence technique, based on human-face studies. In the Emplifi data a person on screen in the first 3 seconds adds only **+3.6% at 3 s**, against +11.7% for speech ([Emplifi](https://emplifi.io/resources/facebook-reels-data/)). The gain belongs to the voice, not the face. So the voice stays on frame one and the presenter's face moves to second 3 or later, or stays off screen until the body of the reel. A 2026 agency newsletter makes the same point as opinion: a sentence delivered to camera is the weakest of the four hook layers (visual, audio, text, verbal) ([Refunnel](https://refunnel.beehiiv.com/p/the-first-0-5-seconds-hooks-that-are-actually-stopping-thumbs-in-q2-2026)).

Text stays light. On speech-led Reels, text overlay is tied to **-6% retention at 10 s** ([Emplifi](https://emplifi.io/resources/facebook-reels-data/)). Lieu's preferred alternative to a title card is to fuse the title into the scene, as on the truck in the Moving Out trailer, and he keeps logos out of the opening or under 1 second each ([Derek Lieu, 2021](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2021/3/29/where-to-put-logos-in-a-game-trailer)). The reel version is one short headline on a banner, podium sign or scoreboard inside the 3D world, plus a name tag per ruler.

One piece of arithmetic shapes every build below. At about 3 spoken words per second (a working value), the full premise sentence runs roughly 8 seconds. It does not fit in the hook. The fix is to let the picture carry the second half: the voice says "I gave two AIs a country each in Roblox" in about 3 seconds while the screen already shows one speaking and one dancing, and a five-word tag ("One gives speeches. One dances.") follows.

## Four ways to build the first five seconds

All timings in this section are design proposals. Frame numbers assume 30 fps. Each build keeps the voice on frame one, avoids black and brightness changes, and never holds a static frame. Build A is the recommendation.

### Build A: the border one-take (recommended)

This combines Lieu's scene lift with the "five shots in one" approach he describes for the Skin Deep trailer, where an opening treated as one take hides several shots ([Derek Lieu, 2025](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2025/7/10/skin-deep-release-trailer)). The orator goes first and the dancer is the turn, which matches the order of the sentence and gives the dance cue a serious beat to contrast with.

| Time | Picture and camera | Character action | Sound | Text |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0-0.5 s (f0-15) | Low medium shot of the orator, 24 mm, camera already pushing in fast. Crowd and flags moving. Cool palette. | Arm already raised, mouth open. No wind-up. | Voice starts on frame 1: "I gave two AIs a country each in Roblox." Crowd bed under it. | Banner in the world carries the headline. |
| 0.5-1.5 s (f15-45) | Push lands with a small overshoot and settles. Light shake on the fist hitting the podium. | Fist slams the podium; 2-3 frame hold on contact; crowd roars. | Transient and body hit on the contact frame, bed ducked briefly. | Name tag pops on the slam frame. |
| 1.5-1.8 s (f45-54) | Whip tilt up and over the border wall, 8-10 frames, hidden cut at peak blur. | None; the camera is the action. | Whoosh starts with the move. "Serious" music halts for a half beat. | None. |
| 1.8-3.0 s (f54-90) | Lands low on the dancer, warm palette. One punch-in on the first beat. | Mid-spin on landing; crowd copies the move one beat late. | Dance cue enters on the landing frame. | Second name tag on the first held pose. |
| 3.0-5.0 s (f90-150) | Boom up and back until both countries sit either side of the wall, scoreboard between them. | Both rulers keep performing, small in frame. | Voice: "One gives speeches. One dances." then the open question. | Scoreboard is the only new text. |

### Build B: stacked split with alternating dominance

In 9:16 a comparison is normally stacked top and bottom, because two panels side by side become very small ([ShortsFast](https://shortsfast.com/blog/split-screen-videos-layouts-examples-and-a-creator-workflow/)). A top/bottom split gives each panel 1080x960. This build shows the whole comparison in frame one, which makes it the best source for a cover frame, at the cost of smaller faces. The staging rule that matters is that two subjects competing equally for attention both fail to register ([3dsense](https://3dsense.net/blogs/what-is-staging-in-animation-guiding-the-viewers-focus)), so dominance alternates.

| Time | Picture and camera | Sound and text |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0-1.5 s | Orator on top, dancer below, both alive. Top panel at full scale and saturation, bottom slightly reduced. Slow push in both panels. The seam is the border wall. | Voice from frame 1. Headline sits on the seam. |
| 1.5-3.0 s | Dominance flips: bottom panel scales up, top eases back. Change by scale and saturation only. | Dance cue takes over from the crowd bed. |
| 3.0-5.0 s | The wall drops or the camera pulls back and the two panels become one shared world shot with a scoreboard. | Tag line and open question. |

### Build C: late state first

The memo's rule for multi-day videos is to cover several days in the opening instead of starting at day one ([memo mirror](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)). Press coverage of Altera's Project Sid, where 1,000+ AI agents ran in Minecraft, led with concrete emergent outcomes such as a priest who bribed townsfolk to convert, not with the setup ([MIT Technology Review](https://technologyreview.com/2024/11/27/1107377/a-minecraft-town-of-ai-characters-made-friends-invented-jobs-and-spread-religion)). This build fits only if the simulation produced a striking state.

| Time | Picture and camera | Sound and text |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0-2.0 s | A late-stage image: one country mid-festival with every citizen dancing, the other packed into a square for a speech. Top-down view swinging to eye level, subject pinned at one screen position. | Voice from frame 1: "This is what happened when I gave two AIs a country each." |
| 2.0-3.5 s | Whip tilt back to day one: two empty plots, two rulers dropping in and landing. Hit-stop, dust and ring on each landing. | Two landing hits, the second varied from the first. |
| 3.5-5.0 s | Push in on the scoreboard at zero. | Tag line and open question. |

### Build D: the minimal re-cut that keeps the presenter

When the presenter shot has to stay, the voice track is kept and only the picture is replaced.

| Time | Picture and camera | Sound and text |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0-1.5 s | Orator full frame, mid-gesture, fast push-in. | Existing presenter voice, first clause, from frame 1. |
| 1.5-3.0 s | Whip tilt to the dancer full frame, matched screen position so the eye does not travel. | Dance cue enters on the landing frame. |
| 3.0-5.0 s | Presenter appears for the stakes line in a moving shot: push-in or short arc, both countries visible behind, face in the upper-middle of the frame. | Open question spoken to camera. |

Three of Lieu's other types are poor fits. The rug pull (a solemn presidential address revealed as a blocky AI) is spoiled by the reel's own caption, which is the failure mode Lieu names. The name reveal costs a second of clarity. The bumper of rapid flashes is the type he calls irritating, and fast cutting "for too long will be exhausting" ([Derek Lieu, 2019](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2019/10/2/editing-an-engaging-trailer-opening)); if used, cap it at 2 seconds.

## The technique catalogue: a few sourced numbers, many starting values

### The numbers that have a source

Published cinematography references describe what each move means and almost never give numbers. StudioBinder's zoom article and Epic's cinematics guide for Fortnite creators contain none ([StudioBinder](https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/what-is-a-zoom-shot-definition/); [Epic UEFN docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/fortnite/making-cinematics-3-camera-movement-and-framing-in-unreal-editor-for-fortnite)). The table below holds every execution number that traces to a source. Most come from editing tutorials and game-development guides read through search summaries, so they are consistent rules of thumb, not standards.

| Technique | Sourced number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Whip-pan transition | 7-10 frames at 24-30 fps, blur peaking at the cut | [FlexClip](https://www.flexclip.com/learn/whip-pan-transition.html), [PremiumBeat](https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/create-whip-pan-using-built-effects-premiere-pro), [MiraCamp](https://miracamp.com/post/whip-transition-in-premiere-pro) |

### Camera moves: working starting values

Everything in this table is an unsourced starting value to tune by eye in Blender. Frame counts read 24 fps / 30 fps.

| Move | What it says | Starting parameters | Main pitfall |
|---|---|---|---|
| Slow push-in | "Pay attention to this" | 3-8% of subject distance per second; near-linear; 24-35 mm for the world, 50 mm+ for a character | Easing both ends makes a slideshow drift |
| Fast "punch" push | Emphasis on a beat | 20-40% of distance in 6-10 / 8-12 frames; ease-out; 2-4% overshoot, settle in 4-6 frames | No overshoot reads as a mechanical tween |
| Crash zoom | Comic or aggressive emphasis | Focal length x1.5-2.5 in 3-5 / 4-6 frames | Reads as a joke and dates fast; 1-2 per reel |
| Whip pan or tilt cut | "Meanwhile", location jump | 4-5 frames out, cut at peak speed, 4-5 in; 60-120 degrees; same direction both sides | Mismatched direction or speed exposes the cut |
| Crane or boom reveal | Scale | Rise 1-3 character heights over 36-60 / 45-75 frames with a slight tilt down | Slow; spend it once |
| Orbit or arc | Hero presentation | 30-90 degrees over 24-48 / 30-60 frames around chest height | A full 360 is a tech demo |
| Parallax truck | Depth | Foreground blocks 0.5-1.5 m from the lens, crossing frame in under 0.5 s | No foreground makes a 2D scroll |
| FPV dive | Speed, geography | 14-20 mm; bank 15-30 degrees; speed peaks mid-dive; pass 0.3-1 m from geometry | Wide lens at low speed is dull |
| Top-down to eye level | Map becomes scene | Swing over 30-48 / 36-60 frames while descending; subject pinned on screen | Subject drift feels unanchored |
| Rack focus | Redirect without a cut | 8-14 / 10-18 frames; f/1.4-2.8 at 50-85 mm; targets 1 m and 5 m+ apart | Hard to see on a phone; exaggerate |
| Dutch tilt | Mischief | 8-20 degrees in landscape terms; about 6-12 in portrait | Under 5 degrees looks like an error |
| Low-angle hero | Power | Knee or hip height, tilt up 15-30 degrees, 18-28 mm | A wide lens close to a block head enlarges it |
| Dolly zoom | The world bending | 12-36 / 15-45 frames; focal ratio 2-3x; focal length driven by distance | One per film; needs deep background |

One easing rule covers most of the table. A move that ends on a hold should ease out with a small overshoot. A move that is cut away from should still be moving, or accelerating, at the cut. Blender's default Bezier keys ease both ends, which suits neither case, so set interpolation per key. Camera anticipation is the companion value: pull 2-5% the opposite way for 2-4 frames before a fast move.

The distinction between a zoom and a dolly matters more in a blocky world than in live action. A zoom is magnification that only creates the illusion of getting closer, while a dolly moves the camera ([StudioBinder](https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/what-is-a-zoom-shot-definition/)). Only the dolly produces parallax, and in a world of grid lines and repeated cubes parallax is the main depth cue. A punch-in done as a 2D scale reads flat; the same punch as a true dolly reads as 3D.

### Portrait changes which moves pay off

Vendor and practitioner guides agree that horizontal movement loses force in 9:16 unless it is a rapid pan, and that vertical frames rely on stacking and on depth ([Reelmind](https://reelmind.ai/blog/vertical-video-cinematography-best-practices-adapting-cinematic-language-to-9-16); [DEV Community](https://dev.to/cartney/vertical-video-composition-for-drama-the-2026-guide-567l)). Blender's Auto sensor fit sizes the sensor from the larger resolution dimension ([Blender manual](https://sources.debian.org/src/blender-doc/4.3-1/manual/render/cameras.rst)). With the default 36 mm sensor at 1080x1920, that produces the geometry below by arithmetic.

| Focal length | Horizontal view in 9:16 | Landscape lens with the same horizontal view |
|---|---|---|
| 18 mm | 58.7 degrees | 32 mm |
| 24 mm | 45.7 degrees | about 43 mm |
| 35 mm | 32.3 degrees | about 62 mm |
| 50 mm | 22.9 degrees | about 89 mm |
| 85 mm | 13.6 degrees | about 151 mm |

A "normal" 35-50 mm already behaves like a telephoto side to side. Showing two rulers or a crowd needs 18-24 mm, or staging in depth and height instead of side by side. As working practice, whip pans become whip tilts, reveals go vertical (tilt-up, boom over a wall, top-down to eye level), push-ins are the most reliable move because they travel along the axis the format does not penalise, and shake is weighted about 2:1 vertical to horizontal.

### Time and impact devices

A speed ramp is best built at animation level in 3D, so motion blur stays correct for the remapped speed. A working shape is 250-400% in, a ramp of 8-12 frames, a hold at 25-40% for 15-30 frames on the key pose, and a 4-6 frame ramp out. The sourced live-action norm of a 1-3 second slow hold is too expensive for a 35-second reel; 0.5-1 second is the budget.

Hit-stop is the best-quantified impact tool and the main replacement for a flash. Game guides stress that attacker and target freeze together, and that only the participants freeze ([Bugnet](https://bugnet.io/blog/how-to-fix-hitstop-or-freeze-frame-feeling-off)). In a rendered reel, hold the contact pose 2-3 frames at 30 fps for a normal hit and 4-6 for the single biggest moment. Keep the camera, dust and text moving during the hold, so it reads as style and never becomes a static frame.

For shake, port the cited trauma model with film-sized values: add 0.3-0.6 trauma at contact, let it decay within 10-18 frames, cap rotation near 1-2 degrees and translation at 1-3% of the frame. The reference constants are tuned for a 2D arcade game and are far too large. Keep on-screen text out of the shaken camera.

The freeze-frame name card needs adapting. The only timing guidance found says such cards should last 2-5 seconds and tire quickly when every character gets one ([PremiumBeat](https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/stylize-video-with-character-title-cards)). That is too long, and a literal freeze breaks the no-static-shots rule. The working substitute is a soft freeze: drop to extreme slow motion with a 2-4% push still running, land the tag on the snap frame, and resume motion in place of a cut. A working budget is 1.2-1.6 seconds per rival, and only the two rulers get a card.

### Lens and image settings

Blender exposes F-stop, blade count and an anamorphic bokeh ratio on the camera ([Blender manual](https://sources.debian.org/src/blender-doc/4.3-1/manual/render/cameras.rst)). The remaining values are working ones. Use a shutter of 0.5 by default, 0.75-1.0 for the few frames of a whip so the cut hides, and 0.2-0.25 where poses must read crisply. On a phone, subtle defocus is invisible: a character close-up needs 50-85 mm at f/1.4-2.0 with the background at least 4-5 times the subject distance. Shallow focus on a wide shot makes a blocky world look like a miniature, so keep wides sharp when the countries should feel big. Keep bloom static per shot, keep any vignette static and elliptical, and use roughly half the chromatic aberration that looks right on a photo, since hard-edged flat colours show fringes strongly.

One choice has to be made once for the whole reel. Spider-Verse turned motion blur off and used a sharper echo treatment in its place ([CG Spectrum](https://www.cgspectrum.com/blog/spider-man-into-the-spider-verse-how-they-got-that-mind-blowing-look)). Arcane's team went the other way and imitated live-action accidents such as handheld shake ([Animation Magazine](https://animationmagazine.net/2024/06/annecy-what-we-learned-at-the-arcane-season-2-session)). Stepped characters and render motion blur conflict, because a held pose has no velocity and the blur flickers on and off. Pick stepped characters with smears and no blur, or full-rate animation with blur.

### Making block characters read as alive

The LEGO Movie is the closest documented reference for rigid block characters. Its animation supervisor describes animating on twos and threes, using no motion blur, never bending arms or legs, and replacing blur with a "brick blur" of stretched bricks because fast motion without it was "too jarring on the eye". Life came from pose, timing and swapped 2D facial expressions ([The Credits](https://www.motionpictures.org/2014/02/how-the-lego-movie-got-its-stop-motion-look/)). Teaching sources name the causes of stiffness as linear interpolation, action with no anticipation or aftermath, and floating contact ([School of Motion](https://www.schoolofmotion.com/blog/new-to-2d-character-animation-here-are-the-most-common-mistakes-and-how-to-avoid-them)).

The working approach is pose, snap, hold: a strong asymmetric key pose, a 2-4 frame transition, one small overshoot, and a hold that still breathes. Offset secondary parts by 2-4 frames per level of hierarchy ([RebusFarm](https://rebusfarm.net/blog/follow-through-and-overlapping-action-the-animation-principle-that-prevents-stiff-motion)). Keep the camera on ones while characters step. At 30 fps the sourced anticipation-move-settle example converts to about 8, 5 and 10 frames, which leaves room for roughly one full action per character inside the hook.

The two rulers have to read as opposites before any text appears. Staging tutorials advise contrasting silhouettes and lines of action for two characters in a shot, and testing each pose as a black shape ([CG-Wire](https://blog.cg-wire.com/staging-animation-principle)). No source addresses rival staging as such, so the application is a design choice: the orator vertical and rigid, gesturing on beats, in a cool palette; the dancer diagonal, off-balance and continuous, in a warm palette.

### Text and safe zones

Third-party safe-zone figures for 1080x1920 conflict ([Hopper HQ](https://www.hopperhq.com/blog/instagram-reel-size/); [PostSyncer](https://postsyncer.com/tips/instagram-reels-size-and-safe-areas)). A box that satisfies every figure found keeps critical text at least 270 px from the top, 340 px from the bottom and 48 px from each side. Check it in the app's own preview. A working text slam scales to rest in 2-4 frames with a 10-20% overshoot and settles in half the attack time, landing on the stressed syllable. A word naming something should never appear before the thing itself; one vendor guide notes that an early caption spoils a spoken punchline ([Sonilo](https://sonilo.com/blog/guides/funny-sound-effects-for-videos)).

### Sound that carries the impact

Trailer-sound sources agree on recipes and give almost no numbers. A hit is three time-aligned layers: transient, body and sub. A riser peaks exactly on the hit. A beat of near-silence before a hit makes it feel larger, and the bed ducks for a fraction of a second under each hit ([Violet Recording](https://violetrecording.com/sound-design-for-trailers/); [Ableton](https://www.ableton.com/en/blog/learn-how-to-make-high-impact-sounds-for-movies-and-trailers/)). Align the transient, not the start of the file, to the action frame ([Morphic](https://morphic.com/resources/how-to/how-to-sync-sound-effects-to-video)). Sources disagree on whether early or late sync is more tolerable, so the safe practice is the contact frame, one frame late at most, checked by ear on a phone speaker.

Three results from the earlier research set the limits. Keep the voice at least 10 LU above music ([Fraunhofer](https://publica.fraunhofer.de/entities/publication/67a38de8-7a6d-422b-93b4-180552acfd89)). Vary the accents, since the orienting response to a repeated effect fades after the third repetition ([Potter et al.](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6634355/)). And a drop to silence before a key line helped recall in radio ads when the gap stayed under 3 seconds ([Olsen 2002](https://asu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/salient-stimuli-in-advertising-the-effect-of-contrast-interval-le)). For the dance gag, the trailer recipe and comedy practice point the same way: halt the serious music a half beat before the reveal, then bring the dance cue in on the reveal frame ([Sonilo](https://sonilo.com/blog/guides/funny-sound-effects-for-videos)).

## Every disliked device has a motion-based replacement

The client's four dislikes map onto specific techniques, and each has a substitute that keeps the punctuation. The flash limit gives a concrete test for the first one. W3C defines a general flash as a pair of opposing changes in relative luminance of 10% or more of the maximum, and allows no more than three in any one second ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html)). A single flash frame is inside that limit, so the objection is taste and brief, not safety. The working rule for this client is zero large-area luminance jumps.

| Avoid | Why it fails here | Replacement |
|---|---|---|
| White or inverted impact frames, dip-to-white, light leaks, strobing beat cuts | Full-frame luminance reversal | Hit-stop, punch-in and small shake; a hue-only accent at similar brightness |
| Animated bloom pops, glow pulses, vignette pulses, full-width white text slams on a dark frame | A brightness flash by another route | Static bloom and vignette; colour accent on one word; local dust or a mid-value ring |
| Dip-to-black; pass-through cuts via an unlit interior | A cut through black | Whip-tilt cut; lit coloured object wipe; pass-through on a lit mid-tone block face |
| Crossfades and preset glitch or RGB-split transitions | Stock overlay unrelated to the scene | Straight cut, or a match cut on shape, motion or colour at matched luminance |
| Opening on a finished static set, or a slow drone drift | No onset, no subject | Camera already moving on frame 1, chasing or revealing a subject; the set assembling |
| Literal freeze frames; ease-in-out drift on a still scene | Dead frame | Soft freeze with a running push; hit-stop with camera and particles alive |
| Linear keys, no anticipation or settle, mirrored neutral poses | Stiff characters | Pose, snap, hold with a breathing hold; offset secondary parts |
| Stepped characters under render motion blur, or a stepped camera | Blur flicker and judder | One family for the whole reel; camera on ones |
| Punch-in on every word; repeated crash zooms; one whoosh reused throughout | A tic that fades after three repeats | About one punch per 2 seconds at most; 1-2 crash zooms per reel; varied accents |

Lieu's own practice supports the replacements. Summaries of his writing say he sticks mostly to straight cuts smoothed by match cuts and eye-trace, though that specific post was read only through a search summary ([Derek Lieu blog](https://derek-lieu-yrd2.squarespace.com/blog/2023/1/7/what-game-trailers-can-learn-from-film-history)). The memo offers a precedent for building the opening from arrival instead of a finished state: in "100 Days in the Circle" the house is craned in at 30 seconds, not sitting there from the start ([memo mirror](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)).

Accents built from the world's own material are the safest route to a premium read, with the LEGO brick blur as the cited precedent ([The Credits](https://www.motionpictures.org/2014/02/how-the-lego-movie-got-its-stop-motion-look/)). Block-shaped dust, a ground ring that characters occlude, and a 1-2 frame smear need no luminance change. Which accents look premium and which look cheap has no citable ranking; that judgement is craft opinion.

Restraint is part of the craft. A GDC Europe 2014 talk argues against reflexively adding juice to everything ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1020861/Don-t-Juice-It-or)). In a 5-second hook with two introductions, give the full stack (hold, shake, dust, ring, tag pop, three-layer hit) to one moment, most likely the dance reveal, and lighter versions to the rest.

## Seven changes that fix a told-not-shown presenter hook

The current hook fails three items of the weak-hook checklist at once: correct words, no visual proof, no open question. The changes below are ordered by expected value, weighing how much of the audience each one reaches. The ordering is a judgement, since none of these has a measured effect on this format.

| # | Change | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Replace the presenter in frame 1 with both rulers in action. Keep the voice on frame 1 and move the face to second 3 or later. | Memo "show, don't tell"; speech carries the measured gain, the face does not |
| 2 | Shorten the spoken hook to one 3-second clause plus a five-word tag, and let the picture carry the talents. | Proposition by 3 s, hook by 6 s ([TikTok Ads Help](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices?lang=en)); full sentence runs about 8 s |
| 3 | Add stakes by second 5: a scoreboard or border, and a spoken question about which country does better. Do not show the winner. | Lieu's question test; curiosity peaks at a specific gap |
| 4 | Start every shot already in motion and join the two rulers with a whip-tilt hidden cut. No black, no dissolve. | Sourced 7-10 frame whip; client brief |
| 5 | Give each ruler one anticipation-snap-settle with a 2-3 frame hit-stop, and make their silhouettes and palettes opposite. | LEGO and Spider-Verse practice; staging contrast |
| 6 | Put the headline inside the world and cut all other text to two name tags. | Text overlay negative on speech-led Reels; Lieu's diegetic titling |
| 7 | Put one sound hit on each contact frame, vary them, and halt the music a half beat before the dance cue. | Netflix sync tolerance; habituation after three repeats |

Build D applies changes 1, 2, 4 and 7 without re-recording the voice. Build A applies all seven.

## Conclusion

The research changes what "premium" means for this hook. It does not mean more effects. The documented high-end references got their look by removing things: no motion blur, no bent limbs, mostly straight cuts, one or two frames of smear. What they added was timing precision, in the form of holds of a few frames, cuts hidden in real motion, and sound on the exact contact frame. That makes the client's constraints an advantage. A hook with no flashes, no black and no static frames is close to what the LEGO and Spider-Verse teams built by choice.

Two things remain unknown, and both can be settled cheaply. No shot-by-shot record exists for the openings of comparable AI-simulation or civilization-experiment videos, so logging the first 5 seconds of ten of them by hand would test these builds against the genre. And every parameter marked as a working value needs an on-device check after upload, because phone screens and platform compression change what reads. Instagram's trial reels, shown only to non-followers, are the direct way to compare Build A against the current presenter opening, with the skip rate and the retention chart as the measure.
